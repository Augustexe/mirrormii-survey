# Genii first ICP and opening hypotheses

Status: **proposed** · 2026-09-18

## Decision to test first

Do not begin with “find out what is wrong with you.” It is a strong click hook, but it promises diagnosis and invites harmful self-pathologising that this survey cannot substantiate.

Test one narrower initial cohort instead:

> **English-speaking North American adults 21–25 who are socially surrounded but privately unconvinced they belong, and who are trying to understand why connection, momentum, and basic routines feel harder than they appear to be for everyone else.**

This is an initial creative/launch hypothesis, not a prevalence claim and not an eligibility rule. It includes all genders. It does not presume loneliness, a mental-health condition, bad friendships, dating status, employment, or a health condition.

The emotional job is not “diagnose my flaw.” It is: **“Name the friction I have not been able to explain.”**

## Why this is a defensible first hypothesis

- In a nationally representative 2024 US survey reported by Pew in 2025, 22% of adults under 50 said they felt lonely or isolated all/most of the time; 38% of all adults said sometimes. The evidence supports a broad connection tension, not an assertion that a particular 21–25 person is lonely. [Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/social-trends/2025/01/16/men-women-and-social-connections/)
- The same Pew reporting identifies younger age, lower income, lower education, and being unpartnered/living with an unmarried partner as correlates of more frequent loneliness. These are research associations, not routing inputs or claims about any respondent. [Pew emotional well-being detail](https://www.pewresearch.org/2025/01/16/emotional-well-being/)
- Deloitte’s 2025 global Gen Z/millennial survey found financial security, meaning and well-being tightly linked in respondents’ reported experience. Its sample is global and workforce-oriented, so it supports testing money/work pressure as a current-chapter branch, not estimating a North American 21–25 prevalence. [Deloitte](https://www2.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/topics/talent/2025-gen-z-millennial-survey.html)
- Harvard’s Spring 2025 Youth Poll of US 18–29-year-olds found that a reported sense of belonging separated respondents’ recent experience sharply; the same study names financial security and finding a long-term partner as salient goals. It supports belonging as a testable first wedge and money/love as selectable branches, not claims about every participant. [Harvard Youth Poll](https://iop.harvard.edu/youth-poll/50th-edition-spring-2025)
- The Federal Reserve’s 2025 household well-being report shows the economic picture for 18–29-year-olds is heterogeneous: 63% reported doing at least okay/living comfortably, while material pressure indicators remained meaningful for a minority. Use money pressure only when selected or directly reported. [Federal Reserve SHED](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/files/2025-report-economic-well-being-us-households-202605.pdf)
- A small 2025 treatment-seeking young-adult study found participants reported online mental-health content influencing self-diagnosis. It is not population evidence, but it makes “find out what is wrong with you” a particularly poor product promise. [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40253960/)
- The US Surgeon General’s social-connection advisory frames connection as a public-health issue, but it does not license a product to diagnose loneliness or claim a companion fixes it. [HHS advisory](https://www.hhs.gov/sites/default/files/surgeon-general-social-connection-advisory.pdf)

## The first-five experience: recognition before classification

The questions should behave as five small, testable predictions about the participant’s **current friction**. Each answer changes the language and the next scene. They must include “none of these / something else / skip,” and no answer is scored as a defect.

| Beat | What Jeannie is testing | Example prompt direction | What it can route to |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Current friction | Which area feels least explainable lately? | “Which tab has been quietly draining your battery lately: people, work/money, dating, body/routine, or something harder to name?” | A chapter branch, not a trait |
| 2. Surface vs private experience | Is the problem absence of people, lack of ease, pressure, or depleted capacity? | For people: “When you are with people lately, what is the part nobody would guess from the outside?” | Belonging, social energy, conflict, or recovery branch |
| 3. Specific pattern | What concrete scene repeats? | “You get home after being social. What happens next before the real you clocks out?” | Direct report of decompressing/routine, not a diagnosis |
| 4. Desired answer | What does the person most want explained or changed? | “If Jeannie could make one confusing thing less confusing tonight, would it be why connection feels hard, why you feel behind, why your body feels off, or something else?” | The result’s promise and the next-question priority |
| 5. Conversation permission | What tone and depth earns a continued conversation? | “When something is tender, do you want gentle wording, clean directness, a little absurdity, or room to describe it first?” | Rapport controls, not personality classification |

After question 2 or 3, Jeannie can make one bounded reflection: “You are not saying you have no people. You are saying being around them has not reliably felt like landing anywhere.” Only show this when the selected responses literally support it, and show it as a checkable reflection—not an authoritative discovery.

## Four launchable creative territories

Use one as the first paid/organic creative hypothesis, not four audiences within one first quiz.

1. **“I have people. Why do I still feel outside?”**
   - Best match to the proposed ICP and companion promise.
   - Opening friction: belonging versus visibility.
   - Health bridge: social depletion, sleep/recovery, eating/routine under pressure—only through direct questions.

2. **“I am functioning. Why does everything feel harder than it looks?”**
   - Broad but still concrete; captures hidden strain without clinical framing.
   - Opening friction: effort-to-output mismatch.
   - Health bridge: rest, routine continuity, movement, meals, emotional recovery.

3. **“I do not know whether I want to leave—or whether I am just tired.”**
   - Career/money chapter for early-career participants.
   - Opening friction: work meaning, security, time, recognition.
   - Must have an equivalent branch for users not in paid work; never assume employment from age.

4. **“Why do I keep wanting closeness, then disappearing when it is available?”**
   - Strong emotional recognition but higher risk of overclaiming.
   - Use as a relationship-pattern creative after evidence and safety review, not the default universal opening.
   - Must not diagnose attachment style or predict relationship outcomes.

## What the evidence bank must add

The existing evidence model is compatible with this direction. It needs an explicit **current-friction layer**, distinct from personality and health:

```text
current_chapter: selected topic(s), explicit, time-windowed where appropriate
felt_friction: direct self-report, no pathology label
repeated_scene: actual event/routine, with no-example option
desired_insight: what the user hopes to understand
rapport_permission: tone/depth preference
reflection: authored inference with supporting source IDs, contrary evidence, version, confidence
```

Do not infer “isolated,” “anxious,” “depressed,” “avoidant,” “burnt out,” “unhealthy,” or “high standards” from a selected opening branch. Those require direct self-report or substantially more specific evidence; the product is not a diagnostic test.

## Validation before locking the ICP

Run a small, sequential discovery test rather than declaring the segment from intuition:

1. Make 4–6 short social creatives, one claim per territory, with no mental-health diagnosis language.
2. Measure hook-stop, comment language, landing completion, first-three-question completion, and the participant’s selected desired insight. Do not infer identity from clicks.
3. Conduct 12–15 moderated interviews among 21–25 English-speaking North American volunteers. Ask what sentence felt uncomfortably true, what felt generic, and what they hoped would happen after completion.
4. Promote only the territory that produces both strong recognition and an expectation MirrorMe can honestly fulfill. A high click rate paired with “I expected therapy/diagnosis/a soulmate” is a failed promise.
