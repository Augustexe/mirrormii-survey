# Jerry's taste: the brief for every card proposal

The standing brief for the card review loop. Every proposal in a `BATCH-*.md` is written against this page first, then against LAUNCH-SPEC section 2 and the card-writer skill. Add to it after every batch; never delete a line without Jerry's say-so (mark it superseded instead).

## What he loves

- **Funny absurd premises that read in one go.** The pumpkin curse ("every winter, one sibling spends a week as a pumpkin") and a year as a goldfish both stayed because they are funny and instantly pictured. Absurd is not the problem; slow absurd is.
- **Specific, slightly embarrassing real moments.** The ex's parent texting "Nothing bad!" two years after the breakup (C1-126, "pretty funny"); the quiet room you booked for your own review versus a coworker's four-minute job interview (C5-141); slept through your best friend's moving day (C2-142, "really good").
- **Answers with a concrete comic detail.** "The stairwell has great acoustics." "It smells like fries." "The groveling starts at minute two." "Send it to the group chat for a ruling first." Each answer is a distinct move with its own joke.

## What he rejects

- **Realistic rewrites that went boring.** The apartment abroad (X-L1-40: "a lot better, but a little bit boring"), family tech support (C6-110: "bland and boring"), the emergency fund (C4-142: "concept boring, answers boring"). Real is only good when it stings or embarrasses; plain adult life admin is not a card.
- **Draining the fun out of absurd cards.** The audit's WEAK_ABSURD flag was wrong for his taste. He kept both absurd originals over their realistic rewrites. Fix a weak absurd card's execution, keep its premise.
- **"Your person."** "I really hate the 'your person' thing." Settled 2026-09-30 (BATCH-02 pass, delegated): "your partner" everywhere, and a text thread names its sender in the prompt; LAUNCH-SPEC rule 25 and the checker ban "your person".
- **Confusing lines.** One odd line kills a card: "these hands are closed on weekends", "couch, phone off" in a ranking (C7-147: "just confusing overall").
- **Thin two-option cards whose answers don't read.** C5-51: "only two option answers and they don't read as legible." He wants 3 to 5 clear options where the choice matters.
- **Bland answers.** Every answer must be legible, distinct and funny or sharp. A sensible middle answer with no joke is a bland answer.

## Working rules drawn from the verdicts

1. One premise, one beat. If it needs "and then" twice, it is two cards.
2. Keep his kept premises; improve execution around them.
3. Every answer passes: can you picture it, is it a different move from the others, does it have a detail or a bite?
4. Realistic proposals need a sting (money, pride, being chosen by default, an awkward text); otherwise go absurd.
5. When a card measures something serious (family duty, savings), the joke carries it; the card never lectures.
6. Say it in fewer words than the last version.
