# Genii thesis evidence allocation and acquisition checklist

Status: research and evaluation plan; no questionnaire, scoring, dashboard or company-system changes. September 18, 2026 UTC. Proposed quotas below are research budgets, not approved route changes or scientific sufficiency thresholds.

## 1. Thesis to test, not assume

For some 21–25-year-old English-speaking North American adults, social access coexists with effortful belonging in particular contexts. A playful, context-sensitive conversation may help them recognize a behavioral tension, inspect its evidence, and choose a useful continuation with Genii/Miia. Directly reported routines can enrich that portrait. Whether this improves recognition, prediction, acquisition or ongoing value requires separate evidence.

Do not turn this into: all young adults are lost; insecurity causes poor health; five questions reveal a personality type; a compelling reading validates a clinical or causal claim; vulnerability predicts willingness to buy.

## 3. Evidence bank: four lanes that must not be merged

1. **Participant evidence:** versioned literal answers, circumstances, target, timeframe, evidence type and permission. Supports this participant's bounded reading.
2. **Research evidence:** primary studies and measurement papers with design, sample, limitations, supported claim and forbidden extrapolations. Informs questions and hypotheses.
3. **Audience-language evidence:** dated public language, voluntary interviews and authorized first-party comments. Informs hooks; does not label authors or establish prevalence.
4. **Validation/delivery evidence:** frozen predictions, blinded evaluations, observed product events, verified handoff and privacy checks. Establishes what our own product does under specified conditions.

No source moves lanes silently. A Reddit phrase is not a clinical construct; a study association is not a personal cause; an Astra agreement is not a human testimonial; a store click is not an install.

## 4. Thesis-linked acquisition checklist

### A. ICP and the initial reason to begin — P0

- [ ] Collect life stage and current friction separately from age/gender.
- [ ] Recruit contrast cases: high access/low belonging; high access/high belonging; low access/wants connection; non-social primary concern.
- [ ] Ask for a recent occasion, what they wanted to understand and what they already tried.
- [ ] Record exact language before showing our hooks, so prompts do not manufacture the problem.
- [ ] Compare actual starts/completion and expectation fit, not just likes or stated curiosity.
- [ ] Record counterevidence: comfortable people may want the quiz for entertainment; uncertain people may dislike the frame.

Academic keywords: `emerging adulthood social connectedness subjective loneliness`, `young adults belonging life transitions`, `social network size perceived social support`.
Audience discovery phrases: `I have friends but feel lonely`, `friend group outsider`, `after graduation friendships`, `new city no close friends`, `everyone's friend nobody's best friend`.
Sources: consented interviews, transparent representative datasets with age/geography breakdowns, dated public comments sampled beyond top-liked posts. Avoid treating campus convenience samples as all 21–25-year-olds or targeting distress as a buying signal.
Decision: keep, narrow or abandon the initial cohort based on problem recognition AND fulfillable app value.

### B. Context-specific belonging and protective behavior — P0

- [ ] Identify the actual group/person: close friend, newer group, work, family, dating.
- [ ] Ask what occurred and what the person did, not only what they would do.
- [ ] Ask why: enjoyment, logistics, politeness, fear of exclusion, habit, obligation or another reason.
- [ ] Collect a contrasting context where the behavior does not occur.
- [ ] Ask whether the outcome matched the feared outcome; do not infer other people's motives.

Academic keywords: `state belonging within person`, `relationship specific attachment variability`, `self presentation motives belonging`, `contingent self worth social approval`, `rejection sensitivity interpersonal behavior`.
Audience phrases: `always the planner friend`, `if I stop texting first`, `performing in friendships`, `I feel like I have to be useful`.
Example: “Last time you took over planning with that newer group, was it mostly fun, easier than waiting, or a way to make sure you had a place? Something else is allowed.”
Claim gate: action plus directly reported motive plus context and counterexample review. These are proposed authoring gates, not validated item-count thresholds. Do not diagnose attachment or rejection sensitivity from custom items.

### C. Insecurity → meaning → chosen goal — P0

- [ ] Offer an optional tender-point question, not a compulsory flaw confession.
- [ ] Separate the concern from its feared meaning: appearance versus “people will not want me there.”
- [ ] Collect trigger, behavior and perceived impact separately.
- [ ] Ask what would feel different if this became easier; let the user select the goal.
- [ ] Ask whether Genii should reflect, brainstorm, gently challenge or leave it alone.
- [ ] Record boundaries around memory, joking and later follow-up.

Academic keywords: `contingencies of self worth`, `self discrepancy actual ideal self`, `autonomous motivation goal selection`, `self efficacy mastery experiences`.
Audience phrases: `I feel replaceable`, `I need reassurance but don't ask`, `I don't want to be a burden`, `confidence without validation`.
Example: “When that insecurity gets loud, what do you worry it says about you?” Follow with “What would you like to feel more able to do?”
Decision: a private user-selected continuation, not a promise to solve insecurity. More disclosure alone is not success.

### D. Emotional experience, action and recovery — P0

- [ ] For a selected scene, ask feeling, outward action and recovery separately.
- [ ] Record intensity/duration only if actually asked with comprehensible anchors.
- [ ] Distinguish choosing privacy from distrust; restraint from calmness; directness from harshness.
- [ ] Include an alternative account and a “not sure” path.

Keywords: `emotion regulation experience expression daily diary`, `expressive suppression interpersonal context`, `affective recovery experience sampling`, `rumination versus problem solving`.
Example: “You said ‘all good.’ Was it actually all good, or did your internal review need another business day?” Literal answer choices must not bundle anger with concealment or a recovery time.
Decision: earn a behavioral contrast such as Resting Nice Face only when internal frustration AND restrained expression are reported across relevant scenes.

### E. Social/emotional context ↔ routine bridge — P0 if retained in thesis

- [ ] Link both observations to the same event/day, rather than joining unrelated monthly answers.
- [ ] Record timing: social event, feeling/behavior, bedtime/meal occurrence, next-day energy.
- [ ] Ask alternative contributors without presuming them: work schedules, noise, illness, travel, caregiving, chosen leisure, relevant voluntary body context.
- [ ] Sample ordinary days and exceptions, not only bad days.
- [ ] Collect repeated within-person records before describing a recurring association.
- [ ] Keep associations and causes separate even after repeated observations.

Keywords: `ecological momentary assessment social stress sleep`, `daily diary belonging sleep within person`, `lagged affect sleep bidirectional`, `bedtime procrastination chronotype work schedule`, `daily stress meal timing within person`.
Example: “Thinking of that evening specifically, did bedtime change? If so, what kept you up?”
Decision: support “on the evenings you described…” only with linked records. A cross-sectional population paper cannot fill a missing personal event link; diary correlations alone cannot prove causation.

### F. Literal health/routine dashboard — P0

- [ ] Sleep: independently collect timing, duration, variation, restoration and usual/recent windows. A usual band is not every night's duration.
- [ ] Eating: distinguish timing, appetite, access, convenience, intake and goals. Takeout is not a nutritional verdict.
- [ ] Skin: distinguish care frequency, self-reported comfort/concern and measured condition. Routine does not establish skin quality.
- [ ] Movement: distinguish days, duration, type and intensity; collect only what the intended display needs.
- [ ] Hydration/body: drink-break attention is not volume or adequacy. Keep sensitive body context optional and purpose-limited.
- [ ] Document missingness for each axis/window, not only a pooled confidence label.

Keywords: `Consensus Sleep Diary`, `self reported sleep duration actigraphy agreement`, `meal timing dietary assessment validity`, `physical activity self report accelerometry agreement`, `patient reported skin symptoms measurement validity`.
Decision: keep current descriptive cards; acquire validated measurement and appropriate specialist review only if moving toward condition/quality assessment. Do not collect photos, diagnoses or wearable records just because available.

### G. Recognition, humor and the feeling of being understood — P0

- [ ] Compare personalized interpretation against a generic plausible reading and an accurate literal recap.
- [ ] Keep tone/length comparable so writing quality does not explain all preference.
- [ ] Randomize presentation and avoid claiming any control is a diagnosis or scientific verdict.
- [ ] Ask what is specifically correct, wrong, missing and surprising, with supporting examples.
- [ ] Separate fun, recognition, trust, creepiness, usefulness and perceived certainty.
- [ ] Compare funny versus plain versions for comprehension and changes in answer selection.
- [ ] Review result text for agreeing with every answer, generic flattery and contradiction suppression.

Keywords: `Forer personal validation fallacy`, `Barnum effect personality feedback`, `perceived partner responsiveness feeling understood`, `personalized feedback generic control`, `humor questionnaire response bias`, `cognitive interviewing response options`.
Decision: determine whether contextual synthesis adds value beyond repeating answers in charming language. “Feels accurate” alone is not prediction validation.

### H. Prediction beyond recap — P0

- [ ] Specify target: unseen hypothetical option, later self-reported event, or independently observed outcome.
- [ ] Freeze train/test membership, mappings, exclusions, baseline and predictions before outcomes.
- [ ] Withhold tuning participants from final evaluation; prevent paraphrased train/test leakage.
- [ ] Match context/resources/target person when evaluating transfer, and test deliberately changed contexts separately.
- [ ] Make choices mutually distinguishable; q58 warmth/directness overlap must not be repaired after seeing outcomes.
- [ ] Report participant count, eligible answers, attempted predictions, abstentions, missingness and matched baseline.
- [ ] Treat repeated answers within a person as clustered, not independent people.
- [ ] Measure calibration only if an actual probability is defined; source-count confidence is not a probability.
- [ ] Use model judges for fidelity with human adjudication samples and independent evaluators; do not call synthetic agreement human accuracy.

Keywords: `incremental predictive validity baseline`, `prospective behavioral prediction`, `selective prediction coverage risk`, `calibration categorical predictions`, `LLM as judge self preference bias`, `clustered repeated measures validation`.
Decision: establish incremental value over baseline before using accuracy language. No defensible required sample size until target metric, baseline, expected effect, precision and clustering are specified.

### I. Survey → app → companion continuity — P0 before promising it

- [ ] Ask what the user expects the app to remember and do next, before showing a sales explanation.
- [ ] Verify selected goal/tone/context can be transferred with explicit permission.
- [ ] Test actual first app interaction against the survey promise; verify skip, deletion and restart semantics.
- [ ] Distinguish CTA click, store visit, attributed install, first open, successful authorized handoff and meaningful return.
- [ ] Collect voluntary return reasons, mismatch complaints and perceived pressure.
- [ ] Evaluate useful support and user control, not emotional dependency or session length alone.

Keywords: `conversational agent perceived responsiveness`, `AI companion longitudinal randomized trial`, `human AI relationship dependency`, `onboarding expectation disconfirmation`, `self disclosure conversational agent privacy`, `cross device onboarding continuity`.
Decision: narrow CTA to verified delivery. Company context route failed in this turn; direct Lark was not refreshed. This checklist does not claim canonical app capabilities are unavailable or verified.

### J. Claim substantiation and sensitive-data boundaries — P0 before public pilot

- [ ] Inventory exact ad, question, result, dashboard and CTA claims, including overall implied meaning.
- [ ] Record evidence required for each claim and review proposed jurisdiction/age scope with qualified counsel.
- [ ] Do not use entertainment/fortune-teller disclaimers as a substitute for substantiation.
- [ ] Separate private answer export from a public allowlisted share card.
- [ ] Minimize insecurity/health collection; specify purposes, access, retention, deletion and optional memory.
- [ ] Do not turn sensitive disclosures into ad-targeting traits or pressure to retain the companion.

Keywords: `FTC Health Products Compliance Guidance implied claims`, `FTC AI companion consumer protection`, `Canada deceptive marketing general impression`, `Canada meaningful consent sensitive personal information`.
Decision: qualified review of the actual experience, not a guarantee against being sued.

## 5. Proposed evidence record template

One row per claim-to-evidence relationship, not one row per interesting link:

- Evidence ID; linked thesis/claim/question IDs; lane.
- Literal observation or narrow source claim; proposed interpretation separately.
- Source URL/path, date accessed, study/publication date or question version.
- Population, age, location, recruitment, context and time window.
- Source type: hypothetical, recalled event, routine report, diary, observed event, study, public language or synthetic output.
- Target relationship/group; event ID if linking observations; study/participant provenance.
- Supporting and contradicting evidence; plausible alternatives; duplicate/paraphrase cluster.
- Design, sample and uncertainty where applicable; limitations and generalizability.
- Permitted output; explicitly forbidden extrapolation.
- Consent/sensitivity, intended use and retention boundary.
- Status: candidate, inspected, supports bounded claim, contradicted, missing or retired.
- Next collection action, decision owner, acceptance criterion and review date.

Keep this as an authoring proposal, not an unrequested Lark schema. Store no real private responses in shared research notes or Git.

## 6. Recommended next allocation

Do not add all missing items to every route. Within a future approved opening budget, prioritize current friction; named context; actual action; optional motive; counterexample; chosen goal and rapport permission. Use conditional follow-ups and reconcile replacement slots before implementation. Context and question quality matter more than simply increasing the bank.

**First discovery pass (proposed):** 12–16 consenting adult moderated sessions across the four contrast groups, approximately 3–4 each. This is a usability/problem-discovery budget, not a powered sample or population estimate. Record item comprehension, Other/no-example patterns, claim objections and expected app value.

**Bridge feasibility pass (proposed):** a separate opt-in 7–14-day brief diary with minimal same-day context/routine items, if the cross-domain hypothesis remains a priority. Its purpose is to test measurement feasibility and identify alternative accounts, not establish causes or power an efficacy claim.

**Before larger prediction/marketing tests:** prespecify metric, baseline, success/failure threshold, recruitment, missingness, participant-level analysis and sample-size justification. Use new people and locked copy after exploratory revision. Do not publish paid campaigns or collect data under this planning authorization.

**Research acquisition priority:** context-specific belonging; perceived responsiveness/generic feedback controls; actual-event motive/counterexample measurement; within-person routine linkage; then companion continuity and claim/privacy review. Add external sources only when they inform a concrete question, claim gate or evaluation decision.
