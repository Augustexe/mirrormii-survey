## Design ideas retained

1. Purpose of attending differs from enjoying social interaction. An event's practical value and familiar company can change the answer.
2. Recognition, usefulness, individual goals, comparison and resources are distinct; noticing resource differences does not imply envy.
3. First feeling when criticized differs from subsequent action once criticism is verified. Tone, context and correction can matter separately.
4. Anchor relationship questions to one chosen person; preserve different targets separately rather than calculating a global attachment type.
5. Helping action and helping motive need distinct evidence. Assess urgency/capacity rather than treating every refusal or favor alike.
6. Inner emotion, outward behavior and later repair are separate.
7. Ambivalence/overlapping options may expose poor question design, not inconsistent personalities. Ask for context rather than forcing a false choice.
8. Reflective value, option fit and voluntary friend-challenge interest are product-evaluation data, not personality evidence.
